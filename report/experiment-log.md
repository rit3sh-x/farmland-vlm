# Experiment log

Every change to the models or their evaluation after cycle 2, with the reason, the measured effect and the decision.
The primary metric is the **four-question held-out mean**: exact-match accuracy averaged over presence, identify, extent
and location, on the held-out fold's test sample. Recipes are chosen on fold 0's inner validation (1,000 tiles, the
same items for every trial) and only the chosen recipe is scored on the held-out folds, so the test sample never
selects anything. The per-trial table is generated by `scripts/study_table.py`; two models compare item by item with
`scripts/compare_runs.py`.

## 2026-11-04: evaluator review

- **What the evaluator asked:** BLEU and ROUGE, hyperparameter tuning of both LoRA and BLIP with every change documented, and a target of 0.98.
- **BLEU and ROUGE:** already computed by every scoring run (BLEU-1/4, ROUGE-L, CIDEr), but not yet tabled.
- **Is the ~68% plateau a metric artefact?** Mostly no. Partial credit gives half for an extent one size off or a neighbouring cell, and the identify set overlap. It lifts cycle 1's five-fold mean only from 66.8 to 71.5. Extent gains the most, 61.0 to 75.0. Identify (56.5 to 58.6) and location (59.6 to 62.3) barely move: their misses are wrong anomalies and far cells. **Decision:** report partial credit beside exact match, and look for gains in the model.
- **Why 0.98 is out of reach on held-out tiles:**
  - The answers come from the expert masks. Dedicated segmentation models reach about 0.55 mIoU on this dataset (4th challenge runner-up, 0.547).
  - Borderline items (about 12% of extents, about 10% of locations) earn 38% credit against 74% on clear items.
  - Accuracy on training tiles could approach 0.98, but it measures memorisation and is not reported as a result.
- **The cycle-1 sweep left gaps the study fills:**
  - Rank rose monotonically up to the top of its grid (VQA 0.405 at rank 4, 0.502 at rank 64), so 128 and 256 were never tried.
  - Alpha (fixed at 2×rank), dropout (fixed at 0.05) and LayerNorm (frozen) were never varied.
  - Every input was 384 px, though tiles are 512 px.
  - all_linear was tried only at LR 5e-4, which derails long runs.
  - Each trial was 10k pairs on 300 validation tiles, so 1 to 3 point effects sat inside the noise.

  **Decision:** a one-change-at-a-time study on fold 0 at 30k pairs, scored on 1,000 validation tiles:
  - input size 512
  - BLIP-large
  - rank 128 and 256
  - NIR false colour (NRG)
  - all_linear at LR 1e-4 and 2e-4
  - trainable LayerNorm
  - alpha 1× and 4× rank
  - dropout 0, 0.1 and 0.2
  - a learning curve from 30k to 480k pairs
- **Qwen3.5-2B:** after cycle 2 it validates at about 0.67, the same as BLIP-LoRA, at about 9× the parameters. It stays as the served model for the laptop app (free-form questions, NDVI input). The BLIP study comes first.
- **Leakage guard:** training now asserts that no field is shared between training, validation and the held-out fold.
- **Provenance:** every run records its commit, a dirty flag, the statistics hash, package versions, GPU and host.
- **Fold ensembles:** never used. Fold k's test tiles are in the other folds' training data.
- **Observability:**
  - `train.jsonl` every 50 steps: loss, gradient norm per tower, loss scale, throughput and GPU memory.
  - LoRA update norms per tower at each validation.
  - Alerts on non-finite losses, overflows and validation collapse.
  - A profiler window: Chrome trace, flamegraph stacks and top operators.
  - Trackio dashboards with GPU and CPU monitors.
  - Per-item predictions at every validation, so any two checkpoints or trials compare item by item.
- **Check of the comparison tool:** cycle 1's fold-0 DoRA against LoRA gives −0.47 points (95% CI −1.21 to +0.29), with 315 items fixed and 352 broken. That matches the report's "no difference".

## 2026-11-04 evening: the workstation, profiled

- **Machine:** svm-ws, an RTX PRO 5000 Blackwell with 48 GB, shared with other users. Jobs run at nice 19 with 4 threads per process.
- **Throughput and memory**, one BLIP-base run of the reference recipe (rank 64, attn_qv, batch 16):

  | Setting | Pairs/s | Peak GPU |
  |---|---|---|
  | 384 px (reference) | about 85 (about 5× a T4) | 8 GB |
  | 512 px | 38 to 53 | 19.4 GB |
  | BLIP-large, 384 px (rank 8 smoke run, shared card) | about 50 | 7.9 GB |

- **PyTorch profiler, 10 steps:**
  - About 104 ms of GPU work per step against about 170 ms of CPU.
  - dtype casts between the fp16 model and the fp32 LoRA weights take 23% of GPU time.
  - The attention softmax backward takes about 14%.
- **py-spy CPU flamegraph** (`report/profile/cpu-flamegraph.svg`):
  - The main process spends 22% launching the forward pass and 17% waiting at the loss-scaler sync while the GPU works.
  - Each of the two DataLoader workers is busy only about 16% of the time.
- **Decisions:**
  - Data loading is not the bottleneck, so preprocessing stays on the CPU. That also keeps pixel values identical to the graded runs.
  - One run leaves the GPU partly idle, so the study runs in three parallel slots.
  - The batch size stays part of the recipe.
- **Memory planning:** the 512 px and BLIP-large trials share one third of the study list, so they never run beside each other and Qwen training on the 48 GB card. `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments` limits fragmentation across the three processes.
- **Download fix:** Hugging Face's `hf_xet` client downloaded at about 45 KB/s from this machine, while plain HTTP through the CDN got about 10 MB/s. The jobs run with `HF_HUB_DISABLE_XET=1` and a 60 s read timeout, so stalled transfers resume.
- **Live dashboard:** Trackio runs on the workstation (`trackio show --project farmvlm-study`, over an SSH tunnel). Hosting it on a Space needs a paid Hugging Face plan. Three concurrent writers never blocked a log call, since writes are queued.

- **NRG doesn't help BLIP.** Putting near-infrared in place of blue is not better, and probably slightly worse. **Decision:** keep RGB.

- **Resolution:** 5,098 paired items give about ±1.2 points of 95% interval, about four times tighter than the old 300-tile validation.
